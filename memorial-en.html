Descriptive Memorandum of the Organizational Data Stewardship Framework

Preliminary version for expert assessment

1. Presentation to the participant

This memorandum presents the Organizational Data Stewardship Framework developed within this research. Its purpose is to provide the information required for participants to examine the artifact components and answer the evaluation questionnaire.

The framework was developed to organize the relationship between Data Governance guidelines and the ongoing activities of defining responsibilities, coordinating across areas, recording decisions, referring occurrences, and monitoring data assets.

The assessment does not examine implementation of the framework in a specific organization. Participants are expected to assess the clarity of the components presented, the coherence of their relationships, and their perceived applicability in different organizational settings.

2. How to use this material

The material may be read in three steps:

  1. Understand the overall framework architecture, composed of two functional layers, five cross-cutting dimensions, and three organizational levels;
  2. Examine the roles, operating flow, and records envisaged for handling data-related occurrences;
  3. Analyze the hypothetical example and answer the digital questionnaire, composed of Likert-scale items and open-ended questions.

The consolidated framework specification accompanies this memorandum as a reference document. It provides further detail on the components, decision rights, limits of action, records, and organizational adaptation possibilities.

3. Problem addressed by the framework

Organizations depend on data for their operational activities and decision-making processes. However, the existence of policies, standards, and decision rights is not sufficient to ensure that these guidelines are applied consistently in daily activities. In complex structures, data circulate across business domains, technical functions, and different decision-making bodies, which may lead to unclear responsibilities, divergent interpretations, and monitoring difficulties.

The framework is based on this gap between Data Governance guidelines and their execution. Its purpose is not to replace Data Governance, Data Custody, or Data Curation. It organizes the Data Stewardship function as a practice of coordination, responsibility definition, occurrence referral, and recording of data-related decisions.

4. Overall architecture

The framework is organized around two types of elements: its functional structure, formed by two layers, and the elements that cut across its operation, represented by five dimensions. It also considers the strategic, tactical, and operational levels at which data-related decisions and activities may occur. The operational architecture presented in this memorandum derives from the preliminary theoretical model underlying the framework and presents its layers, dimensions, organizational levels, roles, flows, and records in an accessible form for expert assessment.

Normative Direction Layer: Data Governance brings together the principles, policies, standards, priorities, decision rights, and accountability criteria defined by Data Governance. It establishes the institutional boundaries within which decisions and responsibilities are exercised.

Coordination and Operationalization Layer: Data Stewardship brings together the activities through which Data Stewardship links those guidelines to business domains, technical functions, and occurrences identified during data processing and use. This layer includes communication among participants, problem referral, monitoring of actions, and decision recording.

The two layers are crossed by five dimensions: Roles and Decision Rights; Sociotechnical Coordination; Accountability and Duty of Care; Integrity, Quality, and Traceability; and Organizational Adaptation.

The strategic, tactical, and operational levels indicate where decisions, coordination, and activities are carried out. They do not correspond to mandatory positions or a single hierarchical relationship. An occurrence identified at the operational level may require tactical coordination and strategic decision-making.

5. Framework dimensions

5.1 Roles and Decision Rights

This dimension defines the participants involved in data-related activities, their responsibilities, decision rights, and limits of action. Its purpose is to prevent decisions from being made without defined authority or one function from assuming competencies assigned to another body.

5.2 Sociotechnical Coordination

This dimension organizes the articulation among business domains, technical functions, and Data Governance bodies. It addresses communication among participants, shared data definition, issue referral, and coordination of occurrences involving more than one area.

5.3 Accountability and Duty of Care

This dimension establishes the elements required to identify who participated in a decision, which criteria were used, which actions were defined, and how the decision may be examined later. It also provides for review when new information, a change of context, an error, or a substantiated challenge arises.

5.4 Integrity, Quality, and Traceability

This dimension addresses data identification, meaning, origin, transformations, rules of use, and known limitations. Integrity, quality, and traceability are related but distinct: integrity concerns consistency and reliability; quality concerns fitness for use; and traceability concerns the ability to reconstruct a data item’s trajectory.

5.5 Organizational Adaptation

This dimension enables the framework to be applied in centralized, federated, or hybrid structures. Adaptation may change role names, number of participants, composition of collegial bodies, records, and approval levels. However, identification of responsible parties, limits of decision rights, coordination mechanisms, occurrence records, monitoring of actions, and the possibility of review must remain in place.

6. Roles considered in the framework

The framework distinguishes the participating functions and bodies in order to avoid overlap of responsibilities.

Body or function Role in the framework
Data Governance Defines principles, policies, standards, priorities, decision rights, and accountability criteria.
Data Stewardship function Translates guidelines into practices, coordinates responsibilities, monitors occurrences, and refers decisions.
Data domain owner Defines meaning, purpose, business rules, quality requirements, and conditions of use for domain data.
Data Custody Maintains data availability, storage, access, protection, and technical integrity.
Data Curation Records context, provenance, metadata, transformations, and reuse conditions.

Data Stewardship is an organizational practice. Its function may be performed by a formally designated data steward, by participants distributed among domains, or by a collegial body. The choice depends on the organizational structure, but decision rights and limits of action must remain defined.

7. Summary operating flow

The flow organizes the handling of data-related occurrences. It does not replace technical, legal, security, privacy, compliance, or audit processes. When an occurrence involves any of these matters, it must be referred to the appropriate institutional function.

Stage Summary of the activity
Identification and recording The occurrence is described, affected data and domains are identified, and a person responsible for monitoring is defined.
Initial analysis Meaning, origin, impact, applicable rules, previous decisions, and required competencies are examined.
Decision or referral The competent body decides the issue or refers it when it exceeds its decision rights.
Execution and monitoring Actions, responsible parties, deadlines, and evidence of execution are recorded and monitored.
Verification and closure Completion of actions is verified, and outstanding issues or remaining limitations are recorded.
Review The decision may be reconsidered if new information, a change of context, an error, or a substantiated challenge arises.

The flow allows returns between stages. An analysis may require new information, a decision may be reviewed, and execution may reveal a new occurrence.

8. Expected records

The framework does not prescribe specific tools. Records may be kept in catalogs, glossaries, ticketing systems, document repositories, or other mechanisms adopted by the organization. The minimum content expected is:

Record Main information
Asset identification Name, description, domain, responsible party, source system, and classification.
Business definition Meaning, purpose, applicable rules, restrictions, and responsible party.
Occurrence Description, affected data, origin, impact, evidence, and person responsible for monitoring.
Decision Competent body, participants, criteria, decision, and rationale.
Treatment plan Actions, responsible parties, deadlines, status, and evidence of completion.
Provenance and transformation Origin, context, transformations, versions, systems, dates, and responsible parties.
Exception or known limitation Applicable condition, rationale, impact, responsible party, and review date.

9. Hypothetical operating example

Consider a financial institution that uses a predictive model to support the analysis of credit applications. The model was developed with historical transaction data, payment history, and variables related to customer location.

During a periodic review, it is identified that negative model decisions are concentrated disproportionately among certain groups of customers. It is also found that part of the training data has purpose restrictions or conditions of use that need to be reassessed. The situation involves risks related to quality, traceability, responsible data use, and the assignment of responsibilities for model-supported decisions.

The Data Stewardship function records the occurrence, identifies the potentially affected data, models, reports, and decisions, and coordinates the participation of the areas involved. The data domain owner clarifies the purpose of use, business rules, and applicable requirements. Data Curation gathers definitions of the variables used, their metadata, provenance, and recorded use restrictions. Data Custody identifies the systems, sources, transformations, and technical flows related to the data used by the model.

Because the occurrence involves different domains, possible effects on third parties, and matters beyond the competence of a single area, it is referred to the Data Governance body and to the competent institutional functions for privacy, compliance, risk, and legal matters. The decision may determine suspension or review of the use of certain variables, update of recorded conditions of use, review of rules applicable to the model, and definition of required actions.

After the decision, the occurrence records, definitions and restrictions associated with the data, model documentation, evidence used in the analysis, and treatment plan must be updated. If limitations or known risks remain, they must be recorded and communicated to the responsible participants.

10. Evaluation guidelines

After reading this memorandum and consulting the other Kit materials, participants must answer a digital questionnaire consisting of ten closed items and six open-ended questions.

The closed items use a five-point Likert scale:

  1. Strongly disagree;
  2. Disagree;
  3. Neither agree nor disagree;
  4. Agree;
  5. Strongly agree.

The items are organized according to the five framework dimensions. After the items for each dimension, an open-ended question allows participants to record rationales, identified problems, and proposed changes. A final open-ended question allows for general comments and matters not covered by the preceding dimensions.

The assessment must consider the clarity of the components, the coherence of their internal relationships, and the perceived applicability of the framework. Participants are not asked to assess the effectiveness of the artifact in a real organization.

Evaluation limit: this material supports the assessment of the framework’s clarity, internal coherence, and perceived applicability. It does not represent implementation, a case study, or proof of effectiveness.
